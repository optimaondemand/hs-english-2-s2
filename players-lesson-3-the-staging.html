One passage, two readings
Layers in the verse
●
Verse can mean two things at once. A reading is one way of taking the words. When the lines support two readings, a director has to choose one to play and accept what it gives up.
SONNET 130
My mistress’ eyes are nothing like the sun;
Coral is far more red, than her lips red:
...
And yet by heaven, I think my love as rare,
As any she belied with false compare.
Shakespeare, Sonnet 130 (public domain), the first two lines and the closing couplet

A staging choice is a trade
What it adds and what it loses
●
A staging choice adds something the text withholds. It can also lose something the text supplied. A director decides whether the trade is worth making. Here is one exchange, and one choice described in prose.
THE EXCHANGE
My Oberon, what visions have I seen!
Methought I was enamour’d of an ass.
Shakespeare, A Midsummer Night’s Dream, 4.1 (public domain), Titania, waking
THE TEXT SPECIFIES
Titania says what she dreamed. Oberon answers There lies your love. Words, order, and speakers are fixed.
THE CHOICE ADDS
Titania turns her face away and never looks at the sleeper. The staging shows her shame, which the words do not state.
THE CHOICE LOSES
Oberon’s line points at someone. If she never looks, his words have less to land on.

Quick Write 22: Director’s Note
Week 6 · 10 points · turned in as this week’s PDF
●
This is the whole Quick Write 22 assignment. Write it in the box at the end of this section. Your notes above are your raw material. Your teacher scores this box.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Choose one short exchange of two to six lines from your scene. Write 300 to 400 words. State what the text alone specifies for the exchange. Then analyze one staging choice: what it adds that the text withholds and what it loses that the text supplied. Argue whether the trade is worth it.

●
Focus question. Does the staging choice give the audience more than it takes away?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Quote the exchange and name its speakers.
2
State what the words alone specify: the lines, their order, the speakers, and any stage direction.
3
Describe one staging choice in plain words (a gesture, a pause, a distance, a cut).
4
Say what the choice adds that the text withholds, and what it loses that the text supplied.
5
Argue whether the trade is worth it, and give your reason.
6
Use commas, semicolons, colons, and periods on purpose, so a reader hears the pause where your argument turns. Use at least two Word Study words, used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
The exchange is quoted, and what the text alone specifies is stated.
☐
One staging choice is described, with what it adds and what it loses.
☐
The writing argues whether the trade is worth it and gives a reason.
☐
Punctuation is used on purpose to set pauses in the argument.
☐
At least two Word Study words are used accurately, and the writing is 300 to 400 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
What the text specifies, and what the choice adds and loses
4 pts		 States what the words alone fix, then names one gain and one loss of the staging choice, each specific. Mixes up text and staging, or names a gain without a loss. 4
Argument about the trade
4 pts		 Argues clearly whether the trade is worth it, with a reason tied to the exchange. States a preference without a reason, or does not decide. 4
Craft targets
2 pts		 Punctuation sets pauses on purpose and two Word Study words are used accurately; length is on target. Fewer than two of either, or off the length target. 2
Total: 10 pts

Plan the design page
Setting, costume or color, and one line of why
●
The design page shows the theatergoer what your production looks like and why. It holds a setting and a costume or color concept. Each has an image or drawing, a caption, and a one-line rationale tied to your director’s thesis.
SETTING
Caption and credit, then one line: why this place serves the thesis.
COSTUME OR COLOR
Caption and credit, then one line: why this look serves the thesis.
EVERY IMAGE
A caption, a credit, and alt text for a reader who cannot see it.

⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND YOUR DESIGN PAGE
An AI image tool can draw a forest set or a costume from one sentence. It does not know the play, and it often adds details the text never mentions, such as modern objects. If you use an AI concept image, label it on the page and write one sentence critiquing what it got wrong. Using one is optional.

Directions a cast can follow
One action, one verb, one order
●
The Director’s Call is spoken. A cast hears each instruction once, so each step holds one action, uses a precise verb, and comes in the order the cast will do it.
WEAK
Make it more emotional and try to move around a bit.
STRONG
1. Titania, wake and sit up slowly.
2. Look out at the audience, not at the sleeper.
3. Speak your first line at half speed.
4. Stop for two beats before Oberon answers.
Each strong step names who acts, what the action is, and when. The weak line names none of them.

Quick Write 23: Rehearsal Call
Week 7 · 10 points · turned in as this week’s PDF
●
This is the whole Quick Write 23 assignment. Write it in the box at the end of this section. Your notes above are your raw material. Your teacher scores this box.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Script the Director’s Call for the key moment of your scene. Write 6 to 10 numbered spoken instructions in the order the cast will do them, then a delivery plan for pace and pause. Write 200 to 300 words in all.

●
Focus question. Could a cast carry out every instruction, in order, without asking you a question?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Name the key moment: the lines it covers and who is on stage.
2
Write 6 to 10 numbered instructions. Give each one action, in the order the cast will do it.
3
Write a delivery plan: where the pace speeds or slows, where the pauses fall, and where the voice rises or drops.
4
Read the script aloud once. Fix any step a cast could misread.
5
Use the imperative mood and precise verbs (cross, pause, lower, turn) in every instruction, and at least two Word Study words, used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
The key moment is named, and 6 to 10 numbered instructions are in a workable order.
☐
Each instruction holds one action and a specific verb.
☐
A delivery plan sets pace and pauses for particular lines.
☐
Every instruction is in the imperative mood.
☐
At least two Word Study words are used accurately, and the script is 200 to 300 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
Sequenced, followable instructions
4 pts		 Six to ten numbered instructions, each one action with a precise verb, in a workable order. Fewer than six, several actions in one step, or vague verbs. 4
Delivery plan for pace and pause
4 pts		 Names where pace changes and where pauses fall, tied to particular lines. General advice such as “speak clearly” with no particular lines. 4
Craft targets
2 pts		 Imperative mood and two Word Study words used accurately; length is on target. Fewer than two of either, or off the length target. 2
Total: 10 pts
Your Quick Write 23, in full
200 to 300 words: the numbered instructions, then the delivery plan.
