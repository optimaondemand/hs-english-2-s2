Presenting with a Clear Perspective
Speaking · Week 9
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
A clear perspective is the thread that holds a spoken presentation together.
A presentation has a thesis the listener can repeat, evidence in an order the ear can follow, and a delivery that lets the voice carry the argument.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can state a thesis a listener can repeat after one hearing.
2
I can arrange evidence in a logical order and name its source.
3
I can control eye contact, rate, pause, volume, enunciation, and gesture.
📚 PART 1 · THESIS, ORDER, EVIDENCE

A thesis is a claim with a perspective, not a topic. “The Great Gatsby and money” is a topic. “Gatsby’s fortune buys him an audience but never a community” is a thesis: it takes a position a listener can accept or doubt.

📝 A FOUR-MINUTE SPEECH, SKETCHED
1 · ABOUT 30 SEC
Opening
Establishes the question and the speaker’s position.
2 · ABOUT 20 SEC
Thesis
One sentence that states the claim.
3 · ABOUT 60 SEC
Point 1
Claim, evidence, source, link to the thesis.
4 · ABOUT 60 SEC
Point 2
Same pattern, a different kind of evidence.
5 · ABOUT 40 SEC
Counterpoint
The strongest objection and the answer.
6 · ABOUT 30 SEC
Close
Returns to the thesis and states what follows.

Order helps a listener who cannot reread. Common logical orders: strongest point last, cause then effect, or oldest evidence to newest. State the order in the opening, and use signposts such as “first,” “second,” and “the strongest objection.” Evidence is credible when its source is named and the quotation is exact: “Fitzgerald writes” and “the historian shows” give a listener something to trust.

📚 PART 2 · DELIVERY

Delivery is a set of choices, and each can be practiced one at a time.

ElementWhat it doesCommon faultFix
Eye contactConnects the speaker to the listener and signals confidence.Reading from the page.Glance at key words, then look up for the whole sentence.
RateSets how much a listener can take in.Rushing when nervous.Speak slightly slower than feels natural.
PauseMarks a break between ideas and lets a point land.Filling silence with um.Pause after the thesis and before the strongest point.
VolumeMakes the speaker audible and signals emphasis.Trailing off at the end of sentences.Finish each sentence at the volume it began.
EnunciationMakes each word clear.Swallowed endings and run-together words.Slow down on names and terms.
GestureAdds emphasis and holds attention.Fidgeting or no movement at all.Plan one or two purposeful gestures for key points.

The delivery map below marks the closing lines of the novel for a spoken reading. One slash is a short pause, two slashes are a long pause, and bold words take the stress.

Gatsby believed in the green light, / the orgiastic future / that year by year recedes before us. // It eluded us then, / but that’s no matter— / tomorrow we will run faster, / stretch out our arms further … // And one fine morning— // So we beat on, / boats against the current, / borne back ceaselessly into the past.
Fitzgerald, The Great Gatsby, Chapter 9 (public domain), marked for delivery

The pause before “So we beat on” gives the final sentence its weight. Slowing at “past” lets the last word land.

📚 PART 3 · REHEARSING

Rehearsal improves delivery faster than rewriting does. Say the presentation aloud, standing, and time it. Record a practice run and listen back once, then change one thing: a pause, a pace, a word that was unclear. Notes work best as a few key words, not a full script, so the eyes stay free.

⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND PRESENTING
An AI tool can suggest an outline or a way to order points. It cannot judge how a speaker sounds, and it may state facts or quotations incorrectly. A speaker should be able to explain every claim in his or her own words and check every quotation against the source.

KEY TERMS
Thesis: a claim with a perspective. Signpost: a spoken word or phrase that tells the listener where the argument is. Enunciation: clear pronunciation of each word.
