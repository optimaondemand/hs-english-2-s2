How an image builds a mood
Words, picture, feeling
●
A figure of speech says one thing and makes you feel another. Mood is the feeling a passage leaves. To explain a mood, name the figure, name the picture it puts in your mind, and say what feeling those pictures share.
OBERON DESCRIBES A BANK
I know a bank where the wild thyme blows,
Where oxlips and the nodding violet grows,
Quite over-canopied with luscious woodbine,
With sweet musk-roses, and with eglantine.
Shakespeare, A Midsummer Night’s Dream, 2.1 (public domain), Oberon
1
The words
Quite over-canopied with luscious woodbine
2
The picture
A roof of flowers over a hidden bed, sweet to look at and sweet to smell.
3
The mood
Sheltered and sweet on the surface, with a trap beneath: Oberon names the bank to plan a deception.

Trace a word, name a mood
Your raw material for Quick Write 19
●
A word’s history often explains why a poet chose it. Look a word up in a dictionary and read its origin. Report only what the dictionary says.
A MODEL: ONE WORD TRACED
Word: enthralled. Line: So is mine eye enthrallèd to thy shape (Titania to Bottom, 3.1). Origin: from thrall, an old word for a person held in service, which came from an Old Norse word for a servant, with the prefix en-, to put into. Why it fits: Titania did not choose her love, and the flower-juice has put her in a kind of service. New sentence: The crowd stood enthralled as the magician lifted the cloth.

Quick Write 19: Mood Lab
Week 3 · 10 points · turned in as this week’s PDF
●
This is the whole Quick Write 19 assignment. Write it in the box at the end of this section. Your notes above are your raw material. Your teacher scores this box.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Choose one passage of 8 to 15 lines from your scene or from the wood (Acts 2 and 3). Write 250 to 350 words. Name the figurative language you find (at least two examples), explain how the pictures it makes build the mood of the passage, then trace the origin of one word from the passage and use that word in a new sentence of your own.

●
Focus question. Which pictures does the passage make, and what single mood do they build together?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Give the act and scene of your passage, and its first and last words.
2
Name two figures of speech in it (a metaphor, a simile, personification, or an image that appeals to the senses). Quote each one.
3
State the mood in one or two words. Then explain how the pictures build it.
4
Choose one word from the passage. Look it up in a dictionary and report where it comes from. Use the word in a new sentence about something outside the play.
5
Use at least two subordinate clauses (starting with when, while, although, or because) and at least two Word Study words, used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
The passage is 8 to 15 lines and is identified by act and scene.
☐
Two figures of speech are named and quoted.
☐
The mood is stated, and the explanation shows how the pictures build it.
☐
One word’s origin is reported accurately and the word is reused correctly in a new sentence.
☐
At least two subordinate clauses and two Word Study words are used accurately, and the writing is 250 to 350 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
Figurative language and mood
4 pts		 Two figures named and quoted; the explanation shows how their pictures build one mood. Figures are named without explanation, or the mood is stated without the pictures that build it. 4
Word origin and reuse
4 pts		 The origin is accurate and tied to the passage, and the word is reused correctly in a new sentence. The origin is missing or inaccurate, or the new sentence misuses the word. 4
Craft targets
2 pts		 Two subordinate clauses and two Word Study words used accurately; length is on target. Fewer than two of either, or off the length target. 2
Total: 10 pts

A second story inside the first
How a layer works
●
The Mechanicals rehearse a story of lovers kept apart, and later the court watches it. That second story is a layer: it changes how an audience hears the first one. The two stories share a shape, forbidden love and a night meeting, and they differ at the end.
Pyramus and Thisbe (the Mechanicals’ play)Hermia and Lysander (the main story)
What keeps the lovers apartA wall: did talk through the chink of a wallEgeus’s claim and the law: Or to her death, according to our law
Where they meetBy moonlight: Pyramus and Thisbe meet by moonlightIn the wood: And in the wood, a league without the town
How the story endsQuince names a tragedy: The most lamentable comedy and most cruel death of Pyramus and ThisbeTheseus promises marriage: These couples shall eternally be knit
THE COURT WATCHES
The best in this kind are but shadows; and the worst are no worse, if imagination amend them.

It must be your imagination then, and not theirs.
Shakespeare, A Midsummer Night’s Dream, 5.1 (public domain), Theseus and Hippolyta watching the Mechanicals’ play

Quick Write 20: The Play Inside the Play
Week 4 · 10 points · turned in as this week’s PDF
●
This is the whole Quick Write 20 assignment. Write it in the box at the end of this section. Your notes above are your raw material. Your teacher scores this box.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Write one analysis paragraph of 250 to 350 words explaining how one key element of your scene, or the Mechanicals’ Pyramus and Thisbe, adds a layer of meaning to the play as a whole. State the layer in a claim, support it with two quoted lines, and explain how the quoted words create the layer.

●
Focus question. What does this element let an audience understand about the play that the main story alone does not?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Choose the element: a speech, an image, a character, or the Mechanicals’ play.
2
State the layer in one claim: what the element adds to the play’s ideas about law, desire, or order.
3
Support the claim with two quoted lines, each cited by act and scene.
4
Explain how the words of each quotation create the layer. Do not retell the plot.
5
Use at least one parallel pair (two phrases in matching grammatical form) and at least two Word Study words, used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
A single claim names the layer and what it adds to the play.
☐
Two quotations are accurate and cited by act and scene.
☐
Each quotation is explained through its wording, not retold.
☐
At least one parallel pair appears.
☐
At least two Word Study words are used accurately, and the paragraph is 250 to 350 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
Claim about the added layer
4 pts		 A clear claim names the layer and what it adds to the play as a whole. The claim is general, or describes the element without naming a layer. 4
Evidence and explanation
4 pts		 Two accurate, cited quotations, each explained through its wording. A quotation is missing, uncited, or left unexplained. 4
Craft targets
2 pts		 One parallel pair and two Word Study words used accurately; length is on target. Fewer than two of either, or off the length target. 2
Total: 10 pts
Your Quick Write 20, in full
250 to 350 words, as one analysis paragraph.

One sentence for the whole production
Your director’s thesis
●
A director’s thesis is one sentence: what the scene is about, and what your production will bring forward. Every later choice, from the design page to the call, answers to it.
A MODEL, ON A SCENE YOU MAY NOT HAVE CHOSEN
In Act 2, Scene 2, Puck’s mistake with the flower-juice turns Lysander from Hermia to Helena, and my production lets Puck watch the sleepers in plain view, so the audience feels how a small error can overturn desire.
