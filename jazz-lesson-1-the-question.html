4
Study one sentence
Then copy its build
●
A feature opens on a place, not on an argument. Fitzgerald builds a place by stacking detail onto a plain statement. Read how he introduces the Buchanans’ house, then see how the sentence is put together. The novel is free at gutenberg.org/ebooks/64317.
A SENTENCE TO IMITATE
Their house was even more elaborate than I expected, a cheerful red-and-white Georgian Colonial mansion, overlooking the bay.
Fitzgerald, The Great Gatsby, Chapter 1 (public domain)
HOW THE SENTENCE IS BUILT
MAIN CLAUSETheir house was even more elaborate than I expected
APPOSITIVEa cheerful red-and-white Georgian Colonial mansion
CLOSING PHRASEoverlooking the bay
An appositive renames or describes the noun beside it and is set off with commas. Here it tells what kind of house, in three details.
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
Which sentence contains an appositive?
Your imitation: one sentence about a real place you know
Main clause, appositive, closing phrase. Commas around the appositive.
Two sensory details you will use in your scene
A sound, a smell, a color, a texture. Choose the ones a visitor would notice first.

Quick Write 25: A Fitzgerald Sentence
Week 1 · 10 points · turned in as this week’s PDF
●
This is the whole Quick Write 25 assignment. Write it here, in the box at the end of this section. The notes you made above are your raw material. Your teacher scores this box.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Write in three labeled parts. Part 1: one sentence that imitates the Fitzgerald sentence shown in this lesson, about a real place you know. Part 2: a feature-style opening scene of that place, 120 to 180 words. Part 3: a note of 60 to 100 words naming two moves you borrowed from Fitzgerald.

●
Focus question. What can a writer show about a place before making any argument about it?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Choose a real place you can describe in detail, from memory or from a photograph you have seen: a street, a store, a field, a hallway.
2
Write Part 1: one sentence with the same build as Fitzgerald’s. Begin with a main clause, add an appositive that names what kind of place it is, and end with a phrase that widens the view.
3
Write Part 2: the opening scene, 120 to 180 words. Use concrete, specific details. Do not argue and do not use judging words such as beautiful or awful. Let the details do the work.
4
Use at least two appositives, set off with commas, and at least two of this week’s Word Study words, used accurately.
5
Write Part 3: 60 to 100 words naming two moves you borrowed. For each move, point to the place in your scene where it appears.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
Part 1 copies the structure of Fitzgerald’s sentence and describes a real place.
☐
Part 2 is 120 to 180 words of concrete detail with no argument.
☐
Part 3 names two moves and shows where each appears in the scene.
☐
At least two appositives are set off correctly, and at least two Word Study words are used accurately.
HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
Imitation and opening scene
4 pts		 The sentence follows Fitzgerald’s structure, and the scene is 120 to 180 words of concrete, specific detail about a real place. The structure is not matched, or the scene is vague or argues instead of showing. 4
Moves borrowed
4 pts		 Two specific moves are named, and each is located in the writer’s own scene. Only one move is named, or the moves are named but not located. 4
Craft targets
2 pts		 Two appositives and two Word Study words are used accurately; each part is on its word count. Fewer than two of either, or a part is off its word count. 2
Total: 10 pts
Your Quick Write 25, in full
Three labeled parts: Sentence, Opening scene, Moves borrowed.

Choose one symbol
Then find two moments
●
A symbol is a thing that carries meaning beyond itself. The novel returns to a few again and again. Choose one to follow. Below is a first moment for each. Find a second moment by searching the free text for the symbol’s name (Ctrl+F in your browser) and note the chapter.
THE GREEN LIGHT · CHAPTER 1
I glanced seaward—and distinguished nothing except a single green light, minute and far away, that might have been the end of a dock.
Fitzgerald, The Great Gatsby, Chapter 1 (public domain)
THE EYES OF ECKLEBURG · CHAPTER 2
The eyes of Doctor T. J. Eckleburg are blue and gigantic—their retinas are one yard high. They look out of no face, but, instead, from a pair of enormous yellow spectacles which pass over a nonexistent nose.
Fitzgerald, The Great Gatsby, Chapter 2 (public domain)
THE VALLEY OF ASHES · CHAPTER 2
This is a valley of ashes—a fantastic farm where ashes grow like wheat into ridges and hills and grotesque gardens
Fitzgerald, The Great Gatsby, Chapter 2 (public domain)
Where to look next. The green light returns in Chapters 5 and 9. The eyes of Eckleburg return later in Chapter 2 and in Chapters 7 and 8. The valley of ashes returns in Chapters 4, 7, and 8 (search for “ash-heaps” too). If you have not reached a chapter yet, use a moment from the chapters you have read.

Two moments and three questions
What the symbol carries, and what you could research
●
Here is one symbol worked across two moments. Notice that the quotations are short and exact, and that the explanation says what changes between the moments.
A SECOND MOMENT FOR THE EYES
Terrible place, isn’t it,” said Tom, exchanging a frown with Doctor Eckleburg.
Fitzgerald, The Great Gatsby, Chapter 2 (public domain)
A MODEL, ON THE EYES (CHOOSE A DIFFERENT SYMBOL, OR MAKE YOUR OWN POINTS)
Moment 1: the eyes are first described as something with no one behind them. They “look out of no face,” so the stare is real but nobody owns it. Moment 2: Tom treats the sign as a person he can share a frown with. The symbol carries the feeling of being watched in a place where no one is answerable. What changes is Tom: he turns an advertisement into a witness.
Three research questions: Why did advertisers of the 1920s put giant signs along roads? How did the automobile change the land between cities? What did roadside signs replace as a way of speaking to strangers?

Quick Write 26: What the Symbol Carries
Week 2 · 10 points · turned in as this week’s PDF
●
This is the whole Quick Write 26 assignment. Write it here, in the box at the end of this section. The notes you made above are your raw material. Your teacher scores this box.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Choose one symbol: the green light, the eyes of Doctor T. J. Eckleburg, or the valley of ashes. Explain how it carries meaning in two moments of the novel. End with three research questions the symbol raises. Total length: 250 to 350 words.

●
Focus question. What does this symbol stand for in the novel, and what could a researcher learn about the world behind it?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Name your symbol and the two moments you will study, each with its chapter.
2
For each moment, quote a short phrase exactly and explain what the symbol seems to carry there. Say what changes, or what stays the same, from one moment to the next.
3
Describe the symbol in at least two sentences with layered description: a main clause plus one or more clauses that add detail.
4
End with three research questions the symbol raises about the real world of the 1920s. Each question must be one a source outside the novel could help answer.
5
Use at least two of this week’s Word Study words, used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
One symbol is named, with two moments from the novel quoted exactly and explained.
☐
The explanation says what the symbol carries and how it shifts or holds between the moments.
☐
Three research questions are listed, and each one is answerable with sources outside the novel.
☐
At least two layered descriptive sentences and two Word Study words are used accurately.
☐
The writing is 250 to 350 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
The symbol in two moments
4 pts		 Two moments are quoted exactly and explained, and the answer shows what the symbol carries and how it shifts or holds. Only one moment is explained, quotations are loose, or the explanation only retells. 4
Three research questions
4 pts		 Three questions are clear, focused, and answerable with sources outside the novel. Questions are too broad, answerable only from the novel, or fewer than three. 4
Craft targets
2 pts		 Two layered descriptive sentences and two Word Study words are used accurately; length is on target. Fewer than two of either, or off the length target. 2
Total: 10 pts
Your Quick Write 26, in full
250 to 350 words. Name the symbol, explain two moments, and end with your three research questions.
