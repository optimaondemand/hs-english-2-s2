📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
A subordinate clause adds a time, a reason, a concession, or a condition to a main idea.
The opening word of the clause names the relationship. Shakespeare’s speakers use these clauses to fit several ideas into a single sentence, and the marker tells the reader how the ideas connect.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can identify the subordinate clause and the main clause in a sentence.
2
I can name the relationship a clause marker signals: time, reason, concession, or condition.
3
I can combine two ideas with the marker that fits their logic.
4
I can define and use this week’s Word Study words.
📚 PART 1 · MAIN AND SUBORDINATE CLAUSES

A clause has a subject and a verb. A main clause can stand alone as a sentence. A subordinate clause has its own subject and verb but opens with a marker such as when, while, because, although, or if, and it cannot stand alone. It depends on a main clause to finish the thought.

Marker key
when, while: time
because: reason
although: concession (grants a point, then pushes against it)
if: condition
✎ ON THE BOARD · WHAT THE OPENING WORD SIGNALS
When thou wak’st, if she be by, / Beg of her for remedy.
When thou wak’st signals time. If she be by signals a condition. The main clause is Beg of her for remedy.
When they next wake, all this derision / Shall seem a dream and fruitless vision;
When they next wake signals time. The main clause is all this derision shall seem a dream.
While she was in her dull and sleeping hour,
While signals time that overlaps another action. In Puck’s speech, the main clause follows two lines later: a crew of workmen were met together to rehearse.
And are you grown so high in his esteem / Because I am so dwarfish and so low?
Because I am so dwarfish and so low gives a reason. The main clause is are you grown so high in his esteem.
Although I hate her, I’ll not harm her so.
Although I hate her grants a point that the main clause then pushes against: I’ll not harm her.
If you were civil, and knew courtesy, / You would not do me thus much injury.
If you were civil sets a condition, and the main clause states what would follow.

The marker is not interchangeable. Because gives a reason, although grants a point before turning away from it, if states a condition, and when and while place events in time. Using the wrong marker changes what the sentence claims.

📚 PART 2 · WORD STUDY

These six words come from this week’s scenes, where illusion and enchantment are the subject. Each entry gives the sense the play uses.

translated (verb, past participle) · changed in form; transformed
Root: Latin translatus, carried across, from trans- (across) + ferre (carry)
Bless thee, Bottom! bless thee! Thou art translated.
enamoured (adjective) · filled with love or fascination
Root: Old French enamourer, from en- + amour, from Latin amor (love)
Mine ear is much enamour’d of thy note.
enthralled (adjective) · held captive by charm or power
Root: en- + Old English thræl (a slave)
So is mine eye enthrallèd to thy shape;
illusion (noun) · a false appearance that is taken for real
Root: Latin illudere, to mock, from in- + ludere (to play)
By some illusion see thou bring her here;
derision (noun) · contempt shown by mocking
Root: Latin deridere, to laugh at, from de- + ridere (to laugh)
To bait me with this foul derision?
purge (verb) · to cleanse; to remove what is unwanted
Root: Latin purgare, to cleanse, from purus (pure)
And I will purge thy mortal grossness so
🌐 LOANWORD SPOTLIGHT · BORROWED FROM LATIN

English uses this Latin phrase to mean genuine or sincere, as when something is offered without any trick.

bona fide (Latin: “in good faith”)
The theater company asked for a bona fide offer of rehearsal space, not a promise that might be withdrawn.
🔑 KEY TERMS
main clause: a clause that can stand alone as a sentence.
subordinate clause: a clause with a marker word that cannot stand alone.
concession: a point granted before the writer turns away from it, often signaled by although.
