Plan the structure
Lede, nut graf, sections, counterclaim, close
●
An investigative feature has a known shape. It opens on a scene (the lede), states what it is about and why it matters (the nut graf), moves through sections that each carry evidence, answers its best objection, and closes by returning to the opening image. Plan each part here. Then draft in Word.

Two elements that carry evidence
Plan, caption, and rights check
●
Multimedia carries evidence when the argument gets weaker without it. An image, a chart, or an audio clip that only decorates the page does not count. Plan two elements. For each, write the caption that connects it to your claim.
THE PARTS OF A CAPTION THAT CARRIES EVIDENCE
LABELFigure 1
WHAT IT SHOWSOne plain sentence on what is in it
WHY IT MATTERSThe claim it supports
CREDITCreator, title, collection, date
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
Which caption shows the table carrying evidence?
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI IMAGES
An AI image tool can make a picture that looks like a 1920s photograph. It is not a record of what the 1920s looked like, and it cannot serve as evidence of the past. If you use one, label it “AI-generated illustration” in the caption and do not present it as a historical source.
Rights and access. Use items you may legally reuse: public-domain images, items with an open license, or your own work. Check each item’s rights statement and copy its credit line. Add alt text to every image (in Word, right-click the image and choose View Alt Text).

7
Plan and rehearse the Premiere
Five moves, 3 to 4 minutes
●
The feature can be read twice. The Premiere is heard once. It runs 3 to 4 minutes and has one job: leave the audience clear about your question, your answer, and the strongest reason for it. It has five moves.
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
Which delivery choice helps listeners follow a spoken thesis?
Move 1: the hook
A scene or image in one or two spoken sentences.
Move 2: the question and your thesis
Say it the way you would speak it.
Move 3: your strongest evidence
One piece of evidence, in short sentences. Name the source in a spoken phrase, such as “as Nick says in the last chapter.” Do not read out parentheses.
Move 4: the counterclaim, named and answered
Move 5: the close
Return to your hook. End there. Do not add a summary after the close.
●
Pace, pause, volume, eye contact, enunciation, and gesture all count. Look up from your notes often. Say it aloud at least three times before you record. Record on any device you have: a phone, a tablet, a computer webcam, or a school recording tool. Video or audio are both accepted. If your tool saves a file, upload it to the Module 4 Project in Canvas. If it gives you a link, paste the link in the Canvas text-entry box.
